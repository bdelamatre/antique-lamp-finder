### OV-1 — Size gate, replaces §1 "Size gate" (approved 2026-07-27)

Run 1 established that the original 18" floor excluded the exact categories the tier table
calls the realistic entry (LAMP-GUIDE §6 L1). Replacement gate:

| | Overall height | Shade diameter |
|---|---|---|
| **Single lamp** | **14–28"** | **8–18"** |
| **Pair** (each lamp) | **12–28"** | **6–18"** |

- Shade over **20"** remains disqualifying in all cases (unchanged).
- Upper bound of 28" unchanged — above that still overpowers a dresser.
- Palette guidance unchanged: amber, gold, caramel, green, copper. Cold blue-white and stark
  black-and-white still read wrong against warm honey maple.
- **A pair at the working budget still beats a single at the stretch ceiling.**

**Why both floors moved.** The height floor is the obvious one, but the shade floor was doing
equal damage silently: the original gate demanded 10–18" shades while boudoir and desk shades
run **6–10"** (the verified Handel "Sunset Palm" shade was 8.25" × 4"). Lowering height alone
would have left the category excluded via the shade dimension. Both floors had to move
together for the change to have any effect.

**What this admits that v1 banned:** boudoir lamps (12–16"), Handel and Tiffany desk lamps
(14–16"), damascene forms — i.e. the affordable Tier A supply. **What stays excluded:**
genuine accessory-scale pieces under 12", such as the 11" Heintz desk lamp rejected in run 1.

## 1. Makers & marks

### Handel (Meriden, CT, 1885–1936) — Tier A
- **Reverse-painted shades are painted on the interior surface.** Exterior decoration is a
  reproduction tell, full stop.
- Signs the **lower rim of the shade**, usually as `HANDEL` followed by a painter's number
  (e.g. `HANDEL 6688`). Often carries a **cloth label** on the base felt.
- Base underside typically marked `HANDEL` in raised or stamped letters.
- Chipped-ice / "Teroca" textured exterior treatments are period-correct on Handel and are
  *not* the same thing as exterior painting — the decoration still lives inside.
- **Form heights (measured, this run):** desk lamps run **14–15.5"** overall; boudoir lamps
  **12–16"**; table lamps **20–26"**. This matters — see §5 Lessons.

### Muller Frères (Lunéville, France, 1895–1936) — Tier D
- Signature normally acid-etched or engraved on the shade, as `Muller Fres Luneville` or
  `Muller Frères Lunéville`, frequently with `Luneville` on a second line.
- **Known reproduction problem:** since the 1980s, glass has been produced carrying the
  `Muller Fres` signature copying the *correct* 1919 signature style but with **different
  graphism** — letterforms, spacing, and stroke weight diverge from period examples. The
  presence of a plausible signature is therefore not evidence of authenticity for this maker;
  the *rendering* of the signature is the evidence. Demand a high-resolution, raking-light
  photograph of the signature before bidding.
  ([MAISON MERCIER COLLECTIONS](https://www.artdecoceramicglasslight.com/makersartists/muller-freres))

### Tiffany Studios — Tier A
- Base underside stamped `TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK` with a model number.
- Leaded shades carry a stamped **inner rim tag**, not an exterior mark.

### Dirk van Erp — Tier C
- Windmill mark, usually with `SAN FRANCISCO`. Box mark variants date the piece.

### Roycroft — Tier C
- Orb-and-cross mark. On copper it is usually struck into the base underside or the rim.

## 2. Authenticity red flags

Working checklist, additive each run.

1. **"Style", "in the manner of", "after", "inspired by", "attributed"** — reproduction and
   marriage markers. Negative-keyword these in every saved search (§4).
2. **Exterior-painted "reverse-painted" shade** — repro.
3. **Bright, uniform solder on a "period" leaded came** — repro. Original solder is oxidized
   and irregular.
4. **Perfectly uniform glass** in a period leaded shade — repro. Period glass striates,
   varies, and bubbles.
5. **Signature in the wrong location or wrong form** — worse than no signature; it means
   someone was trying.
6. **Spelter posing as bronze** — light for its size, gray at chips and wear points.
7. **Shipping economics that contradict the asking price** ⭐ *new this run* — see §5.
8. **Silence in a condition report is a question, not a green light.** Reputable houses
   disclose restoration.

## 6. Lessons learned

### Run 1 — 2026-07-27

**L1. The size gate excludes the categories the tier table names as the realistic entry.**
This is the most consequential finding of the run and it is a **conflict inside the routine
spec itself**, not a market condition.

- §1 size gate sets an **18" minimum** overall height.
- §1 Tier A says *"Smaller Tiffany forms (desk, damascene, boudoir) are the realistic entry"*
  at this budget.
- §1 size gate also says *"Flag matching pairs. Boudoir and small table lamps often sold in
  pairs, and a dresser takes a pair well."*

But measured heights this run: **Handel desk lamps 14–15.5"**, **boudoir lamps 12–16"**,
**Heintz desk lamp 11"**. Boudoir and desk lamps essentially never reach 18". The gate
therefore bans the entire boudoir and desk category — which is precisely the category the
tier table identifies as affordable, and precisely the category §1 asks to be flagged in
pairs. Three of five verified candidates this run died on this gate alone, at prices well
inside the working budget.

Only **table lamps** (20–26") clear the 18" floor, and those are the $2.5–6k+ band — the
stretch ceiling, not the working budget. As written, the routine can only ever buy at the top
of its range.

**RESOLVED 2026-07-27** — owner approved lowering the floor. See **§0 OV-1** for the gate now
in force: single 14–28" / shade 8–18"; pair 12–28" / shade 6–18". Three options were offered
(lower to ~13", keep 18", or split by single-vs-pair); the owner delegated the choice and the
most permissive was taken, on the reasoning that 18" was an estimate while run 1 produced
actual measurements, and that boudoir lamps are by definition dresser lamps. Implementing it
surfaced a second defect in the original gate — the 10" **shade** floor excluded the same
category independently — so both floors moved. Reverting means editing §0 OV-1 only.

**L2. Shipping cost is an authenticity signal.** ⭐ *Added to §2 as red flag #7.*
A Muller Frères lamp asking **$3,250** offered from **Kaunas, Lithuania** with **$16.99
economy shipping** is internally incoherent. Nobody insures, professionally packs, and
internationally ships genuine art glass at that price — $17 is unprotected parcel post. The
shipping line is a claim about what the seller believes the object is worth. When it
contradicts the asking price, believe the shipping line. Combined with the known 1980s
`Muller Fres` signature-graphism reproductions (§1), this listing was rejected without needing
to see the signature at all.

**L3. Fetchability is a hard filter on the whole routine.** Because §4 forbids publishing from
a search snippet, an unfetchable channel produces exactly zero candidates no matter how good
its inventory. Fontaine's (403) is likely the single richest Tier A source in New England —
the June 2026 sale carried 200+ Tiffany lots and a Handel "Poppy" — and it is currently
invisible to this routine. **Solving Fontaine's access is worth more than adding new channels.**

**L4. Channel productivity, run 1.** eBay produced 100% of verifiable candidates. Auction
houses produced schedule intelligence but zero publishable lots, because catalogs for the
next 30 days are either unposted or unfetchable. **Suggested §3 reorder for run 2:** run the
fixed-price sweep (step 4) *first*, then local (step 5), then auction houses (steps 2–3) as
watchlist-building rather than candidate-building.

**L5. Estimates are not comps.** Woody Auction's published pre-sale estimates were the most
abundant price data available this run. They are explicitly excluded from the comp library —
an estimate is a house's marketing position, not a realized sale.
