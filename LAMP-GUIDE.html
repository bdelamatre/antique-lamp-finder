Why both floors moved. The original gate demanded 18″ height and a 10″ shade. Section 1

Makers & marks

Where each maker actually signed. A mark in the wrong place is worse than no mark — it means someone was trying.

Handel Meriden, CT · 1885–1936 · Tier A

  • Reverse-painted shades are painted on the interior. Exterior decoration is a reproduction tell, full stop.
  • Signs the lower rim of the shade, as HANDEL plus a painter's number. Often a cloth label on the base felt.
  • Base underside marked HANDEL, raised or stamped. Patent numbers appear on the shade rim: HANDEL PAT'D NO. 979664.
  • Chipped-ice and “Teroca” textured exteriors are period-correct — not the same thing as exterior painting. The decoration still lives inside.
  • Measured heights: desk 14–15.5″ · boudoir 12–16″ · table 20–26″.

Pairpoint New Bedford, MA · 1880–1938 · Tier A

  • Puffies are moulded, not blown-and-painted. A genuine puffy shade is described as “blown-out” — the relief is in the glass itself.
  • Base stamped with the name plus a model number, e.g. Pairpoint C3064. The number is a good sign of factory work.
  • House estimates run high on puffies. Three of four comparable sales landed under $800 against estimates as high as $2,500. Bid the comps, not the estimate.
  • Boudoir puffies sit at roughly 14.5″ with an 8.5″ shade — inside OV-1, and the sweet spot for this brief.

Muller Frères Lunéville, France · 1895–1936 · Tier D

  • Signature acid-etched or engraved on the shade: Muller Fres Luneville, often with Luneville on a second line.
  • A plausible signature is not evidence for this maker. Since the 1980s, glass has been produced copying the correct 1919 signature style with different graphism — letterforms, spacing and stroke weight diverge.
  • Demand a high-resolution raking-light photograph of the signature before bidding.

Tiffany Studios New York · 1902–1932 · Tier A

  • Base underside stamped TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK with a model number.
  • Leaded shades carry a stamped inner rim tag, never an exterior mark.

Dirk van Erp San Francisco · 1908–1929 · Tier C

  • Windmill mark, usually with SAN FRANCISCO. Box-mark variants date the piece.
  • The market is saturated with “van Erp style” copper-and-mica lamps. Five appeared in a single run-1 sweep.

Roycroft East Aurora, NY · 1895–1938 · Tier C

  • Orb-and-cross mark, struck into the base underside or rim on copper.
  • A listing claiming Roycroft without showing the mark is asking to be taken on trust. Request the photograph.
Section 2

Authenticity red flags

Additive each run. Marked entries were added from a real rejection rather than from reading.

01

“Style,” “in the manner of,” “after,” “inspired by,” “attributed.” Reproduction and marriage markers. Negative-keyword them in every saved search.

02

Exterior-painted “reverse-painted” shade. Reproduction.

03

Bright, uniform solder on a “period” leaded came. Original solder is oxidized and irregular.

04

Perfectly uniform glass in a period leaded shade. Period glass striates, varies, and bubbles.

05

Signature in the wrong location or wrong form. Worse than none.

06

Spelter posing as bronze. Light for its size, grey at chips and wear points.

07

Shipping economics that contradict the asking price. A four-figure art glass lamp offered with $17 economy shipping is incoherent — nobody insures and professionally packs cameo glass at that price. The shipping line is a claim about what the seller believes the object is worth. When it contradicts the ask, believe the shipping line.

08

A title that names the base’s maker but not the shade’s. “Pairpoint base, reverse painted” or “leaded shade with Handel base” is conceding a marriage in the grammar. Four lots in run 1 were caught this way.

09

“On associated base” or “on later associated base.” An explicit, honest disclosure of a marriage. Respect the honesty; still fails the completeness gate.

10

Era language standing in for attribution. “Handel era,” “Handel period.” Names a decade, not a maker.

11

An estimate range so wide it carries no information — $100–$10,000, $1–$1,000. The house is declining to value the lot. Treat as unvalued.

12

Silence in a condition report is a question, not a green light. Reputable houses disclose restoration.

Section 6

Lessons learned

Run 1 · 27 July 2026

L1 · resolvedWhen a gate eliminates everything, suspect the gate

The original 18″ floor excluded the exact categories the tier table calls the realistic entry at this budget. Three of five verified candidates died on it while sitting inside the working budget. Owner approved lowering it; see OV-1.

The part worth remembering: implementing the fix surfaced a second defect. The 10″ shade-diameter floor was excluding the same category independently, so moving height alone would have accomplished nothing. When a constraint is compound, check every arm of it before declaring the fix done.

L2 · resolvedComps live at the auction houses, not the aggregators

Run 1 stalled because eBay’s sold search is login-walled and LiveAuctioneers’ price-guide pages show thumbnails behind a “See Sold Price” gate. The answer was Toomey and Rago, which publish realized prices openly with dates and estimates — four dated Pairpoint sales in a single query. Comp sourcing now leads the run.

L3Fees and premiums decide more purchases than price does

A 28% buyer’s premium plus platform fee and tax multiplies hammer by roughly 1.40. On the one scored lot this run, the difference between bidding $228 and bidding its own low estimate of $650 is the difference between a good buy and a bad one — the lamp is identical.

The same lot bought three ways at Woody costs three different amounts: 15% direct, 20% + 3.99% through the platform, 0% in person. Always read the terms per venue, never per house.

L4House estimates on puffies run high

Three of four realized Pairpoint puffy boudoir sales landed under $800 against estimates reaching $2,500. An estimate is a marketing position. Score against realized comps and treat the estimate as, at most, a signal of what the house hopes.

L5Marriages are usually disclosed in the grammar

Four lots this run named the base’s maker and left the shade unattributed — “Pairpoint base, reverse painted,” “leaded shade with Handel base” — and three more said “on associated base” outright. Reading titles as grammar rather than keywords catches marriages before a page is ever fetched.

L6Reachability beats coverage

Eight of fourteen sources could not be fetched at all. Because nothing publishes from a search snippet, an unreachable source yields nothing however good its stock. Solving Fontaine’s access is worth more than adding three new channels — its June sale carried 200+ Tiffany lots and it is currently invisible.

L7Shipping cost is an authenticity signal

A $3,250 Muller Frères offered from Lithuania with $16.99 economy shipping was rejected without ever needing to examine the signature. The shipping line is a claim about what the seller believes the object is worth; when it contradicts the ask, believe the shipping line.
