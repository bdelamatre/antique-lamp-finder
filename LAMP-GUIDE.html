OV-1 — Size gate · approved 27 July 2026

Overall heightShade diameter
Single lamp14–28″8–18″
Pair, each lamp12–28″6–18″

Shade over 20″ remains disqualifying in all cases. A pair at the working budget beats a single at the stretch ceiling.

Why both floors moved. The original gate demanded 18″ height and a 10″ shade. Boudoir and desk lamps measure 11–16″ with 6–10″ shades, so either floor alone excluded the whole category — the affordable Tier A supply, and the form historically built for a dresser. Lowering height alone would have changed nothing.

Still excluded: genuine accessory scale. The 11″ Heintz desk lamp and the 5.5″-shade Daum boudoir lamp both failed under OV-1, correctly.

Section 1

Makers & marks

Where each maker actually signed. A mark in the wrong place is worse than no mark — it means someone was trying.

Handel Meriden, CT · 1885–1936 · Tier A

  • Reverse-painted shades are painted on the interior. Exterior decoration is a reproduction tell, full stop.
  • Signs the lower rim of the shade, as HANDEL plus a painter's number. Often a cloth label on the base felt.
  • Base underside marked HANDEL, raised or stamped. Patent numbers appear on the shade rim: HANDEL PAT'D NO. 979664.
  • Chipped-ice and “Teroca” textured exteriors are period-correct — not the same thing as exterior painting. The decoration still lives inside.
  • Measured heights: desk 14–15.5″ · boudoir 12–16″ · table 20–26″.

Pairpoint New Bedford, MA · 1880–1938 · Tier A

  • Puffies are moulded, not blown-and-painted. A genuine puffy shade is described as “blown-out” — the relief is in the glass itself.
  • Base stamped with the name plus a model number, e.g. Pairpoint C3064. The number is a good sign of factory work.
  • House estimates run high on puffies. Three of four comparable sales landed under $800 against estimates as high as $2,500. Bid the comps, not the estimate.
  • Boudoir puffies sit at roughly 14.5″ with an 8.5″ shade — inside OV-1, and the sweet spot for this brief.

Muller Frères Lunéville, France · 1895–1936 · Tier D

  • Signature acid-etched or engraved on the shade: Muller Fres Luneville, often with Luneville on a second line.
  • A plausible signature is not evidence for this maker. Since the 1980s, glass has been produced copying the correct 1919 signature style with different graphism — letterforms, spacing and stroke weight diverge.
  • Demand a high-resolution raking-light photograph of the signature before bidding.

Tiffany Studios New York · 1902–1932 · Tier A

  • Base underside stamped TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK with a model number.
  • Leaded shades carry a stamped inner rim tag, never an exterior mark.

Dirk van Erp San Francisco · 1908–1929 · Tier C

  • Windmill mark, usually with SAN FRANCISCO. Box-mark variants date the piece.
  • The market is saturated with “van Erp style” copper-and-mica lamps. Five appeared in a single run-1 sweep.

Roycroft East Aurora, NY · 1895–1938 · Tier C

  • Orb-and-cross mark, struck into the base underside or rim on copper.
  • A listing claiming Roycroft without showing the mark is asking to be taken on trust. Request the photograph.
Section 2

Authenticity red flags

Additive each run. Marked entries were added from a real rejection rather than from reading.

01

“Style,” “in the manner of,” “after,” “inspired by,” “attributed.” Reproduction and marriage markers. Negative-keyword them in every saved search.

02

Exterior-painted “reverse-painted” shade. Reproduction.

03

Bright, uniform solder on a “period” leaded came. Original solder is oxidized and irregular.

04

Perfectly uniform glass in a period leaded shade. Period glass striates, varies, and bubbles.

05

Signature in the wrong location or wrong form. Worse than none.

06

Spelter posing as bronze. Light for its size, grey at chips and wear points.

07

Shipping economics that contradict the asking price. A four-figure art glass lamp offered with $17 economy shipping is incoherent — nobody insures and professionally packs cameo glass at that price. The shipping line is a claim about what the seller believes the object is worth. When it contradicts the ask, believe the shipping line.

08

A title that names the base’s maker but not the shade’s. “Pairpoint base, reverse painted” or “leaded shade with Handel base” is conceding a marriage in the grammar. Four lots in run 1 were caught this way.

09

“On associated base” or “on later associated base.” An explicit, honest disclosure of a marriage. Respect the honesty; still fails the completeness gate.

10

Era language standing in for attribution. “Handel era,” “Handel period.” Names a decade, not a maker.

11

An estimate range so wide it carries no information — $100–$10,000, $1–$1,000. The house is declining to value the lot. Treat as unvalued.

12

Silence in a condition report is a question, not a green light. Reputable houses disclose restoration.

13

Hyper-precise estimates at a multiple of market — $32,175–$41,827, $13,702–$17,812, $21,307–$27,699. Real estimates are round, because they are judgements. Figures carrying four significant digits have been generated, most likely converted from another currency or produced algorithmically. In run 1 every such estimate came from one of the two houses rated do not bid in §4. Treat the pattern as a signal about the seller, not the lot.

Section 4

Auctioneer diligence

Every lot is bought as-is, final, no returns. The house’s honesty is the protection, so who is selling matters as much as what is sold. Ratings below come from published buyer reviews, BBB records and complaint boards, checked 27 July 2026. Re-check any house before bidding — reputations move.

Bidhaus New York, NY

Do not bid

~869 platform reviews · BBB complaints on file · not accredited

  • Cancels winning bids after the hammer, invoking a terms clause covering lots “sold in error by our Auctioneer.” Buyers report hundreds of successful bids voided after close.
  • Bait-and-switch reported: a lot photographed and sold, then the buyer is told it is unavailable and offered “something of equal value.”
  • BBB complaints allege fabricated bidding and that the business “scams people out of money.”
  • Misrepresentation — a modern John-Richard bench sold as antique, needing undisclosed restoration, available new for less.
  • Poor packing; one item shipped in a paper grocery bag. Support unresponsive.

Three Bidhaus lots surfaced in run 1 and all were rejected on other grounds before this research was done. That was luck, not process. Treat the house as disqualifying regardless of the lot.

Mynt Auctions New York, NY

Do not bid

~902 platform reviews · BBB complaints on file

  • Authenticity guarantee not honored. A BBB complaint describes a counterfeit Ferragamo where the buyer invoked the guarantee, supplied verification contacts, and was refused a return.
  • Reported using AI to “prove” authentication in a dispute over a Louis Vuitton bag that smelled of glue and appeared newly made despite the model being discontinued.
  • False advertising on stated sizes; months-long waits between sale and delivery; difficulty getting any response.

Disqualifying for this brief specifically. Everything here turns on a signature, a mark, or a shade being original to its base. A house that will not stand behind an authenticity guarantee cannot be bought from when authenticity is the entire proposition.

Woody Auction Douglass, KS

Trusted

4.9 / 5 · 2,861 reviews · third generation, trading since 1945

  • The strongest reputation in this set. Specialists in exactly the right material — American Brilliant cut glass, art glass, lamps.
  • Descriptions complete and matched to photographs; buyers specifically praise ethics and accuracy.
  • In-house shipping is a stated strength — items arrive intact even when boxes are mishandled. Rare among houses; most of the complaints in this section are shipping complaints.
  • Bid direct. 15% premium on their own platform versus 20% + 3.99% through LiveAuctioneers, and 0% in person.

Toomey & Co. Chicago, IL

Trusted · comp source

~408 platform reviews · trading since 1987 · merged into Rago/Wright 2022

  • Condition reports are accurate and unhedged — buyers note they commit to condition where most houses equivocate. That is the single most valuable trait in a house for this brief.
  • Buyers reporting unbroken satisfaction across decades of transactions.
  • Publishes realized prices openly with dates and estimates — the basis of this guide’s comp library.

Rago Lambertville, NJ

Trusted · comp source

Rago/Wright group · recognized authority in 20th-century decorative arts

  • David Rago is a leading authority on early 20th-century American ceramics and design; the house is a reference point for the period this brief covers.
  • Open realized prices with dates. Paired with Toomey as the standing comp source.

Andrew Jones Auctions Los Angeles, CA

Trusted

~232 platform reviews · predominantly positive

  • Corroborated by their own catalogue. Three of their lamp lots in run 1 said “on associated base” or “on later associated base” — disclosing marriages that many houses bury. Those lots were rejected, but the disclosure is a mark of integrity.
  • Items described accurately; staff responsive; showroom well kept for previewing.
  • Shipping charges repeatedly called excessive. No packing materials provided at pickup — bring your own.

Americana Auctions Rehoboth, MA

Good, with caution

4.6 / 5 · 193 reviews · 40 years trading · holds the run-1 scored lot

  • Predominantly positive: responsive, accurate descriptions, good packing, repeat buyers.
  • One integrity complaint worth knowing before bidding. A buyer reported a completed purchase being withdrawn after another buyer offered more money, alongside a credit-card overcharge. Single report, dated April 2020, against a large base of good reviews — but it is the specific failure that would matter most if it recurred.
  • Another buyer received a rug with active moth infestation — a condition-reporting miss.
  • 28% buyer’s premium is at the top of the normal range and is what pushes the landed multiplier to roughly 1.40.

Not disqualifying. Bid, but get the sale confirmed in writing, pay by a method that can be disputed, and collect in person if possible — Rehoboth is close enough that pickup removes both the shipping cost and the handling risk.

Thomaston Place Thomaston, ME

Good, with caution

~444 platform reviews · widely respected in the trade

  • Quality consignments, good photography, reliable condition descriptions.
  • In-house shipping is the most-complained-about in this set — reported at roughly 3× expected cost with 3–4 week waits, plus poor communication.
  • One buyer argued significant faults should have been caught by the house, with limited photographs masking problems on a 25% fee.
  • Closest geographically. Collect in person and the main complaint disappears.

Section 7

Lessons learned

Run 1 · 27 July 2026

L1 · resolvedWhen a gate eliminates everything, suspect the gate

The original 18″ floor excluded the exact categories the tier table calls the realistic entry at this budget. Three of five verified candidates died on it while sitting inside the working budget. Owner approved lowering it; see OV-1.

The part worth remembering: implementing the fix surfaced a second defect. The 10″ shade-diameter floor was excluding the same category independently, so moving height alone would have accomplished nothing. When a constraint is compound, check every arm of it before declaring the fix done.

L2 · resolvedComps live at the auction houses, not the aggregators

Run 1 stalled because eBay’s sold search is login-walled and LiveAuctioneers’ price-guide pages show thumbnails behind a “See Sold Price” gate. The answer was Toomey and Rago, which publish realized prices openly with dates and estimates — four dated Pairpoint sales in a single query. Comp sourcing now leads the run.

L3Fees and premiums decide more purchases than price does

A 28% buyer’s premium plus platform fee and tax multiplies hammer by roughly 1.40. On the one scored lot this run, the difference between bidding $228 and bidding its own low estimate of $650 is the difference between a good buy and a bad one — the lamp is identical.

The same lot bought three ways at Woody costs three different amounts: 15% direct, 20% + 3.99% through the platform, 0% in person. Always read the terms per venue, never per house.

L4House estimates on puffies run high

Three of four realized Pairpoint puffy boudoir sales landed under $800 against estimates reaching $2,500. An estimate is a marketing position. Score against realized comps and treat the estimate as, at most, a signal of what the house hopes.

L5Marriages are usually disclosed in the grammar

Four lots this run named the base’s maker and left the shade unattributed — “Pairpoint base, reverse painted,” “leaded shade with Handel base” — and three more said “on associated base” outright. Reading titles as grammar rather than keywords catches marriages before a page is ever fetched.

L6Reachability beats coverage

Eight of fourteen sources could not be fetched at all. Because nothing publishes from a search snippet, an unreachable source yields nothing however good its stock. Solving Fontaine’s access is worth more than adding three new channels — its June sale carried 200+ Tiffany lots and it is currently invisible.

L8Vet the house before quoting a max bid, not after

Run 1 computed a max bid on a lot at Americana Auctions before doing any diligence on the house. The diligence came out mostly fine — 4.6 across 193 reviews — but it also turned up a buyer reporting a completed purchase withdrawn after someone offered more. Had that been a pattern rather than a single 2020 report, the bid would have been quoted on a house that does not reliably honour its hammer.

The same pass found two houses — Bidhaus and Mynt — with BBB complaints for voiding winning bids, substituting lots, and refusing to honour an authenticity guarantee. Three Bidhaus lots appeared in run 1 and were rejected on unrelated grounds. That was luck. House diligence now runs before scoring, not after.

L9Every bad house in this set failed on shipping or on honouring the sale — not on fakes

The intuition going in was that the risk is counterfeits. Across eleven houses researched, the actual failure modes were shipping charged at 3× customary (Jackson’s, Thomaston, Andrew Jones), condition not disclosed (Bruneau), and sales not honoured after the hammer (Bidhaus, Americana once). Only Mynt failed on authenticity directly.

This reorders the questions to ask a house. Shipping cost and the terms around a completed sale deserve as much scrutiny before bidding as the mark on the base — and both argue strongly for the standing preference for local pickup.

L7Shipping cost is an authenticity signal

A $3,250 Muller Frères offered from Lithuania with $16.99 economy shipping was rejected without ever needing to examine the signature. The shipping line is a claim about what the seller believes the object is worth; when it contradicts the ask, believe the shipping line.
