Five listings verified live. Under the original gate, all five failed.

The binding constraint was never price or quality. It was the size gate — an 18″ minimum height paired with a 10″ minimum shade diameter, which together excluded boudoir and desk lamps entirely. That is the affordable Tier A supply, and the form historically built for a dresser.

The floors have been lowered (OV-1) and three of the five are back in scope. They still cannot publish as buy candidates, because scoring requires a low comp and the comp library is empty — eBay’s sold-listing search is behind a login wall. Publishing them anyway would mean inventing the denominator.

One blocker remains, and it is comps. Run 2 leads with comp sourcing before any new searching.

“Rare 1900s Roycroft hammered copper table lamp, twin stem”

Tier C $800 BIN / OBO El Segundo, CA

Listed as an original Roycroft rather than “style”, so it passes the keyword filter — but the page gives no dimensions, no orb-and-cross mark, and nothing at all about the shade. The era field says 1920s while the title says 1900s. At $800 it would be far under a genuine example, which cuts both ways.

Ask the seller for: overall height and shade diameter; a close photograph of the base underside and rim showing any mark; confirmation the shade is present and original; and the packing plan — $16.65 ground shipping on a claimed rare Roycroft is itself a warning sign. View listing →
